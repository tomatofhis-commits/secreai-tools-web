Developer's Note

Design Philosophy — Hands-Free Playlist Playback Alongside Your Main Work

Catching up on YouTube subscriptions while coding, researching, or browsing usually means switching tabs repeatedly and re-adjusting volume or playback speed every time a new video starts. YouTube Multi Auto Playlist was created to keep 1 to 4 video players and a smart queue docked inside Chrome's Side Panel so your main workspace stays undisturbed.

Continuous auto-play in a browser panel presents two technical hurdles: memory buildup from recycled video frames over long sessions, and the hassle of scraping dozens of channels manually every day.

In v1.2.0, the extension introduces a 3-stage memory reset (releaseVideo resource cleanup, full iframe destruction, and fresh recreation) paired with a sequential load queue to prevent browser freezes. Combined with batch RSS feed fetching (archiving up to 2,000 videos locally) and automatic volume & playback speed persistence, it delivers a reliable, lightweight companion for daily YouTube viewing.

Key Features

Key Features & Technical Advantages

From 1–4 screen side-panel layouts and RSS feed batching to 3-stage memory cleanup for marathon auto-play sessions.

1–4 Screen Side Panel & Resizable Queue

Powered by Chrome's Manifest V3 sidePanel API—open the player with one toolbar click. Switch between 1 Screen and 4 Screens anytime (hidden frames stop automatically), and drag the divider above the Queue to resize the playlist height freely.

Page Scraping & Batch RSS Fetching (2,000 Archive)

Collect videos directly from your active YouTube tab via Get Videos (with a Stop button to halt scrolling), or register favorite channels with + Ch and fetch their XML feeds in batches of 5 via RSS. Fetched videos merge into a local archive of up to 2,000 entries.

Date, Anchor Title, Unwatched Filters & Sort

Expand the collapsible filter bar to narrow your queue by start date (From:), Anchor title, or Unwatched status. Sort ascending (▲) or descending (▼) by fetch order/timestamp, and tidy up played items with Clear Played.

Auto-Play & Persistent Volume / Speed

With Auto enabled, the extension detects video completion (ended) and loads the next queued video after a 3-second buffer. It also listens to volumechange and ratechange events so your preferred volume, mute state, and playback speed carry over automatically.

Lite Mode & 3-Stage Memory Cleanup

Lite mode (ON by default) uses lightweight /embed/ players with Referer optimization to avoid Error 152-4; uncheck it anytime to fall back to Watch theater mode. Each video transition runs a 3-stage reset (releaseVideo → iframe removal → fresh recreation) through a sequential load queue to keep RAM in check.

Isolated Sub-Frame Rules & Local Storage

Scoped declarativeNetRequest rules apply only to sub_frame contexts inside the Side Panel, leaving your regular YouTube tabs and live chats completely untouched. All playlists, RSS channels, and preferences stay local in chrome.storage.local.

Numbered Control Menu Guide ([1]–[12])

How to Build Your Queue by Goal (Numbered Guide)

Step-by-step workflows using the numbered controls ([1]–[12]) shown above.

Pattern 1: Specific Channel or Playlist

Grab All Videos Without Date or Title Filters

1. Open the target YouTube channel's "Videos" tab or a playlist page.
2. Open the options row with [1] and make sure [8] From: (click × next to it to clear) and [9] Anchor title are empty (enable [10] Unwatched if you only want unseen videos).
3. Click [2] Get Videos to collect the page's videos automatically. Use [3] (▲ / ▼) to sort and [4]–[6] to set screens, Lite, and Auto.

Pattern 2: Favorite Channels × Date Filter

Collect Favorite Channels Back to a Specific Date

1. Open your YouTube "Subscriptions" page (youtube.com/feed/subscriptions).
2. Click [1] to expand the options row.
3. If you want to fetch via RSS, visit your favorite channels and click [11] + Ch to register them (the registered count appears on [12]).
4. Enter your target start date in [8] From: (e.g., 2026/10/06) and optionally check [10] Unwatched.
5. Click [2] Get Videos (or [12] RSS for registered channels) to queue every video published from that date onward.

Pattern 3: Anchor Video Title

Collect Videos Back to a Specific Video Title

Even when date filtering ([8]) does not capture the exact range you expect, setting a landmark video title ensures you reliably collect every video right up to that point.
1. Open your YouTube "Subscriptions" page (or a target channel page).
2. Click [1] to open the options row, and clear [8] using the × button if you do not need a date limit.
3. Type the title (or part of the title) of your stopping-point video—such as the last video you watched—into [9] Anchor title.
4. Click [2] Get Videos (or [12] RSS for registered channels) to gather videos backward until [9] is reached.

FAQ & Troubleshooting

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers on embed restrictions, volume/speed persistence, RSS vs. scraping, memory usage, and privacy.

Q1. What if YouTube shows an embed restriction error (such as Error 152-4)?

If a creator disables external embedding, uncheck Lite in the top control bar. The player will switch to standard Watch theater mode so you can continue watching without embed restrictions.

Q2. Do my volume and playback speed stay the same on the next video?

Yes. The extension listens to volumechange and ratechange events, saves your settings in chrome.storage.local, and automatically restores your volume, mute state, and playback speed when the next video starts.

Q3. When should I use Get Videos vs. RSS?

Use Get Videos when you want to grab videos from the YouTube page currently open in your tab. Use + Ch and RSS when you want fast, repeatable batch updates from your favorite channels without scrolling through YouTube pages.

Q4. Will long auto-play sessions slow down my browser?

Keeping Lite ON minimizes baseline RAM usage, and every video transition runs a 3-stage memory reset (releaseVideo → iframe removal → fresh recreation) through a sequential load queue to prevent memory leaks.

Q5. Does this affect my regular YouTube tabs or live chat?

No. Header rules in background.js are scoped to sub_frame contexts, and UI cleanup runs only inside Side Panel player frames—your normal YouTube tabs and live chats remain unaffected.

Q6. Are my playlists or registered channels sent to external servers?

Never. All playlists, RSS channels, up to 2,000 archived items, and player preferences are stored locally in chrome.storage.local (see our Privacy Policy).
